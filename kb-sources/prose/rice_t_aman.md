# Transplanted Aman rice (T. Aman) — cultivation, fertilizer, and pest management

Transplanted Aman is the main rainfed rice crop of the kharif-2 season in Bangladesh. Seedlings
are raised in a nursery and transplanted from mid-July to mid-August, and the crop is harvested
from November to December. Because it is largely rainfed, Aman suits medium and lowland fields
with good water retention such as clay and clay-loam soils; sandy soils drain too quickly and are
less suitable.

## Fertilizer

Fertilizer for Aman is lighter than for irrigated Boro because rainfall supplies part of the crop's
needs and yields are lower. Apply all of the TSP, MoP, gypsum, and zinc as a basal dose during
final land preparation, together with roughly one-third of the urea. Split the remaining urea into
two or three top-dressings timed to the active tillering, panicle initiation, and (where needed)
early heading stages. Keeping urea out of standing floodwater immediately before heavy rain
reduces nitrogen loss to runoff and leaching.

## Water

Aman relies on monsoon rainfall. Supplemental irrigation is only needed to protect the crop during
dry spells at the critical tillering, panicle initiation, and flowering stages. Draining excess
water before top-dressing improves fertilizer uptake.

## Pests and diseases

The most damaging insect pests of Aman are the yellow stem borer, which causes deadhearts and
whiteheads, and the brown planthopper, which can cause patches of "hopperburn" in dense, heavily
fertilized crops. Scout fields weekly from tillering onward. Avoid excessive nitrogen, which
favours planthoppers, and conserve natural enemies by avoiding unnecessary insecticide sprays.

Common diseases include sheath blight, which spreads in dense canopies and warm humid weather, and
bacterial leaf blight, which worsens after storms and with high nitrogen. Balanced fertilizer,
wider spacing, and resistant varieties reduce disease pressure. Sheath blight is managed by
draining the field, reducing nitrogen, and treating only when lesions climb toward the flag leaf.
