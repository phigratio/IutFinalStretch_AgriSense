# Boro rice — cultivation, fertilizer, and pest management

Boro is the irrigated dry-season rice crop. Seedlings are transplanted from mid-December to the end
of January and the crop is harvested in April and May. Because it is grown when there is little
rain, Boro depends entirely on irrigation and therefore has both the highest water requirement and
the highest yield potential of the rice crops. It performs best on clay and clay-loam soils that
hold water well.

## Fertilizer

Boro needs more fertilizer than Aman because of its higher yield target and the leaching of
nutrients under continuous irrigation. Apply the full TSP, MoP, gypsum, and zinc plus about
one-third of the urea as a basal dose, then top-dress the remaining urea in two or three splits at
active tillering, panicle initiation, and early heading. Zinc deficiency is common in Boro fields
and shows as bronzing of lower leaves; the basal zinc dose addresses it.

## Water

Boro is irrigated throughout the season. The most sensitive stages are panicle initiation,
flowering, and grain filling, when water stress causes the greatest yield loss. Alternate wetting
and drying can save water during vegetative growth without reducing yield, but the field should be
kept flooded around flowering. Cold spells early in the season can cause seedling injury and
spikelet sterility.

## Pests and diseases

Stem borer and brown planthopper are the key insect pests, as in Aman, and planthopper risk again
rises with heavy nitrogen and dense canopies. Rice blast is a more serious disease in Boro because
cool nights and dew favour it; neck blast at heading can cause severe grain loss. Manage blast with
resistant varieties, balanced (not excessive) nitrogen, and, where pressure is high, a protective
treatment at booting to early heading. Sheath blight and bacterial leaf blight are managed as in
Aman through balanced fertilizer, spacing, and drainage.
